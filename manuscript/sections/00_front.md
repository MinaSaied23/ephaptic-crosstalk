**Abstract.** Ephaptic excitation of unmyelinated C-fiber nociceptors by myelinated Aβ afferents at sites of nerve injury has been proposed as a mechanism of tactile allodynia. We tested whether it can reach threshold in a closed-loop core-conductor model. A CRRSS Aβ axon and a C-fiber with Hodgkin–Huxley or phenomenological Nav1.8/Nav1.9 kinetics share a restricted extracellular compartment along a 5-mm lesion. Intracellular and extracellular potentials are solved together, and every endpoint is shown to converge. A single Aβ action potential depolarized the C-fiber by {{NavC_n1_dv2}} mV. Synchronous volleys, equivalent to smaller extracellular cross-sections per fiber, raised this to at most {{NavC_max_dv}} mV; the response was limited not by saturation but by failure of Aβ conduction inside the confined lesion. The depolarization lasted ~{{fwhm_n10_us}} µs, was spatially narrow and was flanked by hyperpolarization, so no fixed voltage threshold applied: a 0.1-ms pulse could drive the Nav1.8/Nav1.9 membrane to {{sd_navc_point_v01}} mV without firing it, and the recorded ephaptic drive had to be amplified {{alpha_navc_n25}}-fold to fire it at 25 synchronous fibers. No C-fiber action potential occurred over five decades of extracellular leak, lesions of 1–7 mm, stimuli of 1.2–20× threshold, 50–400-Hz trains or sensitizing bias currents, and a 0.5-ms onset dispersion removed {{jit_NavC_n25_att0p5}} % of the drive. Faster gating alone had no effect; action potentials appeared only when the whole Hodgkin–Huxley membrane was made faster (time constant ≤ {{kin_hh_speed_min_tau}} ms), never with Nav1.8/Nav1.9 kinetics, whose activation lies {{navc_m8_gap}} mV above rest. Direct ephaptic Aβ→C excitation therefore requires conditions that also block the Aβ source.
